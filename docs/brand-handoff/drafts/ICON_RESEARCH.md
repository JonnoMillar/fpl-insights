# Custom football icons — research and revised approach

29 October 2026. Jonno requested controlled, original image-generated studies
instead of a large sheet of library icons and improvised symbols. Work on one
football concept at a time, review its meaning and silhouette, then resolve
geometry and small-size performance before extending the family.

## Relevant primary examples

- [Ajax iconography, by its designer Gino van Lierop](https://dribbble.com/shots/26859779-Ajax-Iconography-Icons): commissioned by Smörgåsbord, built around the custom typeface's geometry and technical character. The useful lesson is shared construction rooted in the brand; do not copy Ajax's artwork or impose its bevel treatment on FPL Insights.
- [TF Sports iconography, by Diógenes da Silva](https://www.behance.net/gallery/180481645/Iconography-TF-Sports): the designer describes bespoke brand-aligned icons authored in distinct 24px and 16px sizes. A large attractive drawing does not establish that a small UI icon works.
- [Player! Sporticon guidelines](https://github.com/ookamiinc/Sporticon/blob/master/documentation/sporticon-design-guidelines.md): explicit rules for stroke, corners, optical margins, motion direction and spacing between equipment and ball. Their numeric values belong to their system, not ours.
- [BeSoccer / ProFootballDB report](https://pro.besoccer.com/assets/img/landing/pdf_pro/informe_28.pdf): goals/assists graphic uses football and boot symbols respectively. This supports a boot as an existing football convention for assists; it does not establish universal or unambiguous recognition.

## First subject: assists

The previous running-shoe asset with speed marks read as pace. Explore a
recognisable low football boot with studs and a ball, without sprint streaks.
One generated study with a pass arrow was judged too wide and diagram-like
by Codex; a subsequent study removed the arrow and tightened the composition.
Jonno has not reviewed or approved either custom image study yet. No new
production assists SVG has been created, and no small-size pass is claimed.

Custom appearance alone is not enough: the next review should assess whether
the icon suggests football assistance rather than pace, a shot or generic
equipment, and whether its character belongs beside the new dashboard.
Retain an explicit Assists label rather than expecting the drawing to encode
the distinction between a pass, key pass and goal assist by itself.

Penalties and free kicks should subsequently use football-specific evidence
and appropriate context. Do not reinstate the generic target or curved-arrow
drafts merely because their files exist.

Image generation is for exploring original forms; vector construction is
still appropriate after the design is agreed, for clean exports and optical
adjustments. Neither generation nor code makes a design good automatically.
